## 6. Historical failure geometry

Cycles 015–017 collectively show broad failure across compact invented words, pseudo-Latin and pseudo-Germanic forms, open-CV constructions, surname-like forms, rare-language transplants, medical/pharma morphology, fantasy or mythology coding, and AI/SaaS-adjacent identifiers. The repeated structural lesson is that changing phonetic geometry alone does not solve global identity collision.

## 7. Permanent single-word invention freeze

Cycle 018 applied the required freeze. No new standalone invented-name batch was run. The cycle excluded pseudo-Latin, pseudo-Germanic, pseudo-Nordic, open-CV.CV.CV inventions, vowel-initial inventions, `-vex/-yvex`, `-yn`, `-ax/-ex/-ix/-ox`, `-eron/-erion/-ion`, compact 4–9 letter inventions, cosmetic spelling variants, one-letter mutations, and joins of rejected roots.

## 8. Registry-first methodology

The intended priority was USPTO/TSDR or current official U.S. trademark search, EUIPO, WIPO, UK IPO, DJKI/PDKI Indonesia, and relevant national registries in Nice classes 9, 35, 38, and 42. In this run, authoritative trademark-register coverage was not sufficiently accessible for a complete candidate-by-candidate search. Therefore trademark status for all candidates is **UNVERIFIED / REQUIRES PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE**. Public package and developer indexes were queried where accessible, but a zero result is not a legal or global registry clearance.

## 9. Compound/phrasal generation

A deterministic pool of **120 candidates** was generated across six families: infrastructure noun plus action; action plus infrastructure; abstract determinant plus infrastructure noun; uncommon word plus infrastructure noun; two-word phrasal marks; and compounds with a controlled coined component. The pool intentionally used meaningful words rather than another pseudo-word batch. The raw pool was retained as an internal audit artifact and was not treated as cleared naming inventory.

## 12. Company/product funnel

The 15 deep candidates were tested against exact phrase use, fused-form use, company/product identity, adjacent software, consumer identity, and dominant search association. Fourteen were rejected because of an exact active identity, same-category or adjacent infrastructure collision, strong component saturation, consumer/product confusion, healthcare or transport association, or weak master-brand distinctiveness. One candidate advanced conditionally.

Trace Orchard produced observed zero results in the checked exact/fused GitHub, npm, PyPI, crates.io, Docker, and MCP-related searches, but those results are not universal absence proof.

## 14. Cross-lingual evidence

The screen included Indonesian component checks and obvious English/cross-lingual interpretation. Rejected candidates showed meaningful interpretation or association risks: Cinder Relay evokes ash plus hand-off; Bind Harbor evokes binding/difficulty plus harboring; Serein Rail has competing pronunciation and a railway reading; Clear Mosaic and Copper Trace are transparent descriptive phrases; and Steady Trace has a health-monitoring interpretation. Trace Orchard rendered approximately as a fruit-garden trace in the Indonesian component check, with no obvious profane or hostile meaning found. Broader language-by-language clearance remains required.

## 15. Distinctiveness gate

The following candidates failed the whole-mark distinctiveness or category-association gate: Copper Trace, Lantern Route, Mosaic Run, Cinder Relay, Meridian Run, Bind Harbor, Anchor Mosaic, Quiet Junction, Steady Trace, Clear Mosaic, Morrow Lattice, and Serein Rail. Cairn Relay and Relay Morrow also failed because a strong technical component identity made the proposed extensions look derivative or confused. Full market-by-market phonetic clearance was not performed.

## 26. Strategic learning

Compound/phrasal territory materially differs from Cycle 017’s executed single-word territory and was therefore worth testing. It produced one conditional survivor rather than zero, but the early screen also showed that ordinary compounds often inherit strong meanings from their components: healthcare, transport, games, crafts, events, music, consumer products, and established infrastructure software. The next naming effort should not return to unconstrained pseudo-word invention. It should either deepen professional clearance of Trace Orchard, test registry-derived acquisition or brokered marks, or use professional naming counsel to create a legally and linguistically disciplined shortlist.
