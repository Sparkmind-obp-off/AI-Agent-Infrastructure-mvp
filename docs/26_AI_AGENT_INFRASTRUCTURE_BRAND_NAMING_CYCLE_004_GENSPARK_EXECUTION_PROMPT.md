# 4. CYCLE 004 CORE PRINCIPLE — PURE NEUTRALITY

This is the most important new rule.

## 4.1 Neutral means neutral

A candidate is preferred when an ordinary international audience is unlikely to interpret it as belonging to a particular:

- religion;
- deity tradition;
- mythology;
- spiritual practice;
- occult system;
- sacred vocabulary;
- ethnic/religious identity;
- political ideology;
- extremist movement;
- fictional universe;
- fantasy creature/monster category.

The goal is not to erase linguistic origin.

A word may have a documented linguistic origin.

What matters is whether the **brand itself carries a strong non-neutral cultural or religious identity**.

## 4.2 Explicitly prohibited naming territory

Do NOT intentionally generate names based on:

- gods/deities;
- demons;
- angels as religious entities;
- religious figures;
- prophets;
- saints;
- sacred objects;
- scripture terminology;
- prayers/mantras;
- ritual terminology;
- religious cosmology;
- mythological heroes;
- mythological creatures;
- monsters;
- dragons;
- elves;
- orcs;
- demons/undead/fantasy creatures;
- occult terminology;
- astrology/astrological entities;
- esoteric/spiritual terminology;
- religious symbols or sacred titles;
- names strongly associated with a particular faith;
- fantasy-fiction franchises;
- fictional species or characters.

**Do not use obscure mythology as a naming shortcut.**

Obscurity is NOT neutrality.

## 4.3 Cultural neutrality test

A candidate may originate from any language only if research shows that its actual brand perception is sufficiently neutral.

For every root or construction record:

- language;
- literal meaning;
- source;
- ordinary usage;
- cultural context;
- religious/spiritual association;
- mythological association;
- fantasy association;
- personal-name association;
- geographic association.

If the strongest association is religious, mythological, spiritual, fantasy, monster/creature, or otherwise culturally narrow:

**TERMINAL — NON-NEUTRAL**

Cycle 004 is not only about clearance.

- memorable;
- elegant;
- modern;
- confident;
- easy to say;
- easy to repeat;
- not childish;
- not aggressive;
- not fantasy-like.

### Shape

Prefer:

- clean letterforms;
- visually balanced spelling;
- compact length;
- distinctive but not bizarre construction;
- easy capitalization;
- clean logo potential.

### Speech

Test:

- Indonesian;
- English;
- first-hearing recognition;
- telephone/voice recognition;
- meeting-room repetition.

### Memory

Ask:

> If someone hears this once, can they reasonably remember and type it later?

### Brand feel

The desired feeling is:

**premium + elegant + modern + technical + calm + confident + scalable**

Avoid:

**fantasy + gamer + monster + mystical + religious + childish + gimmicky + aggressively futuristic + generic AI startup.**

---

# 7. TARGET NAMING STYLE

Do NOT optimize for “weird enough to be available.”

Optimize for:

> **Distinctive enough to own, simple enough to remember, neutral enough to travel globally, elegant enough to build a premium technology company around.**

Preferred construction families:

### A — Pure coined marks

Invented names with no intended religious/mythological meaning.

The phonotactics should be deliberate and pronounceable.

### B — Neutral linguistic transformations

A real neutral word/root may be transformed into a distinctive brand form.

### C — Neutral multi-root constructions

Two ordinary, non-sacred semantic roots may be combined if the result remains elegant and brandable.

### D — Abstract phonetic marks

A coined mark can be completely semantically neutral.

This is explicitly allowed.

### E — Cross-language neutral construction

Roots from multiple languages may be used only when:

- both meanings are ordinary and neutral;
- the combination is linguistically defensible;
- no cultural/religious identity dominates;
- pronunciation remains simple.

---

# 8. DO NOT CHASE MEANING

Cycle 004 must not assume every good brand needs a dictionary meaning.

A strong master brand may be:

- coined;
- abstract;
- semantically light;
- suggestive rather than literal.

Do NOT downgrade a candidate merely because:

> “It has no dictionary meaning.”

For this cycle:

**brandability > literal meaning**

provided that the candidate has no problematic hidden meaning or cultural association.
