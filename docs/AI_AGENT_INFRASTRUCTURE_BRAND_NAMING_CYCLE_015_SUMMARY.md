## 8. Under-tested Naming Territory

The following territories were **not tested at scale** in Cycle 015 destruction runs. They are noted for future cycles, not endorsed:

- Genuinely short (3–4 letter) closed-syllable roots with terminal stops (e.g. -k, -t, -p). Cycle 015 did not test these because the constitution favors 5–6 letter recoverable roots.
- Compound-of-abstracts with a deliberately non-semantic linker (X-Y-X or A-of-B forms).
- Deliberately non-alphabetic marks (numerals, ampersands) — excluded by the constitution.
- ISO-language-neutral one-syllable coinages heavily tested in the AI-agent space (Gong, Poke, Kit) — excluded here because they occupy a saturated market.

## 9. New Cycle 015 Methodology (materially different from a pure phonetic-aesthetic pool)

**Method:** *Constrained construction grammar with hard-stop enterprise consonants and pre-filter lexicon negation.*

Rules applied to Cycle 015 candidate generation:

1. **Shape:** 5–7 letters, 2–3 syllables, terminal vowel `-a` or `-o` (Indonesian-pronounceable, no `-e` / `-i` drift).
2. **Interior consonant:** must be a hard stop drawn from {t, k, p, d, b} — deliberately borrowing the "enterprise-infrastructure" phonotactic signature of Kubernetes, Kafka, Docker, Postgres, and rejecting the "soft fantasy" liquids (l, r, m, n) as interior consonants.
3. **Ex-ante lexicon negation:** reject before any web search if the root contains:
   - Known pharma class stems: `-mab`, `-zole`, `-pril`, `-olol`, `-tin`, `-cin`, `-sartan`, `-formin`, `-dipine`, `-statin`, `-dopa`, `-vaptan`, `-uro-`, `-oxa-`, `-pen-`.
   - Known Indonesian / Malay lexemes as substring: `data`, `tekno`, `dokter`, `bakti`, `kader`, `udara`, `pikir`, `agen`, `mesin`, `jaringan`, `aman`, `umbi`, `dampak`, `produk`.
   - Latin verb infinitive tails: `-are`, `-ere`, `-ire`.
   - Common European surname tails: `-ovic`, `-escu`, `-eanu`, `-ando`, `-inho`, `-ova`.
4. **Adversarial addition (materially new for Cycle 015):** the destruction loop actively searches for the *worst-case* collision (surname, place, medical, Balkan meaning, Ido/Esperanto word, product-documentation substring) rather than only for direct-match commerce hits.

Why this is materially different: an aesthetic-first phonetic pool produces names that "feel premium" but overwhelmingly collide with real linguistic or commercial identities. Cycle 015 replaces "does it sound premium?" with "does the root fail any of the following disqualifying tests?", and treats *absence of English-language search results* as insufficient evidence of cleanliness.

## 10. Candidate Generation Method

Candidates were generated in three sub-batches by three sub-grammars, each intended to explore a different phonotactic axis:

- **Sub-grammar A (round 1 / 6 tested):** `[C1][V1][C2][C3][V2]` five-letter roots with hard interior clusters (kt, pt, kn, dr, tr, pr, br, kr), terminal `-a` or `-o`. Example survivors of the ex-ante screen: Ovanto, Elutra, Adruto, Idomra, Utrivo, Anektro.
- **Sub-grammar B (round 2 / 6 tested):** `[V][C1][C2][V][C3][V]` VCCVCV six-letter roots with a hard mid-consonant, minimising surname density. Example: Aktomo, Ipkado, Ebakto, Aptumo, Kepodra, and one Idomra retest.
- **Sub-grammar C (round 3 / 5 tested):** rare initial consonants (Z-, X-, V-) plus consonant clusters, deliberately exiting the Latin surname zone. Example: Odrepa, Ipluka, Utrako, Zoktara, Xakotra, Vokranti, Klondura, Pluvarko.

## 27. Strategic Learning

Three learnings that should shape future cycles:

1. **"Search-sparse" is a weak signal.** Nearly every candidate that returned few English-language search hits still had a terminal collision under root/substring, cross-lingual, industrial-documentation, or slang-database inspection.
