# 3. DURABLE NAMING CONSTITUTION

Cycle 004 established a durable rule.

## PURE NEUTRALITY IS A HARD GATE

Do not intentionally generate or retain names whose dominant identity is:

- religion;
- deity;
- sacred terminology;
- scripture;
- ritual;
- spiritual practice;
- occult/esoteric terminology;
- mythology;
- mythological heroes;
- monsters;
- fantasy creatures;
- fictional universes;
- fantasy characters;
- political/extremist identities;
- strongly ethnic/religious-coded terminology;
- dominant personal names;
- dominant geographic names.

**Obscure mythology is still mythology.**

Do not use obscure cultural references merely because they are search-sparse.

A name may have a linguistic origin in any language if its actual meaning and brand association are ordinary, neutral, and commercially usable.

## BRANDABILITY IS ALSO A HARD GATE

The desired brand feel:

**premium + elegant + modern + calm + confident + memorable + technical + scalable**

Avoid:

**fantasy + monster + mystical + religious + gamer + childish + aggressive + gimmicky + generic-AI.**

A coined name with no dictionary meaning is fully acceptable.

Literal meaning is optional.

# 5. CYCLE 005 METHODOLOGICAL SHIFT

Cycle 005 is explicitly NOT another Nordic/Finnic search and NOT another 3-syllable `-ova/-ella/-vana/-venta` style search.

Explore three architecture families in parallel.

## FAMILY A — SHORT COINED MARKS

Target:

- approximately 4–7 letters;
- preferably 2 syllables;
- clean consonant/vowel rhythm;
- easy first-hearing spelling;
- visually simple;
- abstract or semantically light;
- no forced suffix.

Do NOT automatically reject endings previously banned merely because they were banned.

Instead evaluate the **whole mark**.

The old blanket suffix ban is removed.

However, generic startup morphology remains discouraged when it makes a name feel synthetic.

Target feeling:

**simple, sharp, premium, modern.**

Think of the *structural simplicity* of successful modern technology brands, not imitation of any particular brand.

Do NOT copy or create near-neighbors of existing famous brands.

## FAMILY B — TWO-WORD / PHRASAL MASTER BRANDS

Explore combinations of two short neutral words.

Possible semantic territories:

- system;
- layer;
- fabric;
- field;
- frame;
- signal;
- core;
- flow;
- structure;
- runtime;
- network;
- works;
- foundry;
- substrate;
- stack;
- bridge;
- relay;
- lattice;
- engine;
- plane.

These are **territory examples, not candidate instructions**.

The combination must be evaluated for:

- originality;
- neutrality;
- enterprise credibility;
- trademark collision;
- search collision;
- memorability;
- future extensibility.

Avoid obvious generic phrases that would be impossible to distinguish.

Do not blindly reuse saturated terms such as “AI”, “Agent”, “Tech”, “Cloud”, “Labs”, “Digital”, “Systems” as the primary differentiator.

## FAMILY C — SHORT NEUTRAL LEXICAL / SEMANTIC MARKS

Explore real short words when they are:

- ordinary;
- culturally neutral;
- not religious;
- not mythological;
- not fantasy-coded;
- not heavily occupied in software/infrastructure;
- commercially distinctive enough to investigate.

Possible semantic territories include:

- structure;
- continuity;
- precision;
- connection;
- foundation;
- motion;
- signal;
- flow;
- coherence;
- fabric;
- layer;
- frame.

These are conceptual territories only.

Do not automatically use the example words themselves; many are already saturated.
