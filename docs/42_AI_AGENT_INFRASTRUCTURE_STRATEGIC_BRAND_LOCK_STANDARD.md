# AI AGENT INFRASTRUCTURE — STRATEGIC BRAND LOCK STANDARD
## Naming Doctrine v2 — Distinctive Enough, Defensible, Then Build

**Status:** CANONICAL ACTIVE NAMING STANDARD  
**Effective:** 2026-09-21  
**Applies to:** all future AI Agent Infrastructure naming cycles  
**Supersedes:** overly restrictive collision rules in earlier naming prompts where they conflict with this document

---

## 1. WHY THIS STANDARD EXISTS

The previous naming program became too rejection-heavy.

The recurring pattern was:

CANDIDATE → ANY COMPONENT COLLISION → REJECT → NEW CANDIDATE

That can create an artificial requirement that a brand must be globally unused before it is allowed to exist. That is not the operating standard demonstrated by the project's other successful master-brand decisions.

The strategic objective is different:

> Find a brand that is sufficiently distinctive for its intended category, commercially usable, linguistically workable, and defensible enough to justify professional clearance — then build distinctiveness through actual use.

The target is not lexical emptiness.

The target is **commercial distinctiveness with manageable risk**.

---

## 2. WHOLE-MARK FIRST PRINCIPLE

The primary naming object is the **whole brand mark**.

Evaluate:

1. exact whole-mark identity;
2. confusingly similar whole marks;
3. same-category commercial use;
4. dominant existing perception;
5. trademark exposure in intended goods/services;
6. domain/social practicality;
7. pronunciation and spelling;
8. infrastructure brand architecture.

A component being used elsewhere is **not automatically a terminal failure**.

Example logic:

- A common word used by an unrelated restaurant is not automatically a technology collision.
- A common word used by an active AI infrastructure company in the same category may be a material collision.
- A component used in a completely different commercial context should be contextualized, not automatically treated as disqualifying.
- A component with a famous global identity may still create a real risk if it dominates perception of the whole mark.

The question is:

> **Does the existing use materially threaten the intended identity, category, registrability, discoverability, or long-term commercial use of this whole mark?**

---

## 3. COLLISION SEVERITY MODEL

Every observed collision must receive a severity class.

### C0 — NO MATERIAL COLLISION

No meaningful commercial identity conflict found.

Proceed.

### C1 — INCIDENTAL / UNRELATED USE

Same or similar word exists, but the use is clearly unrelated and unlikely to dominate perception.

Do NOT automatically reject.

Document it.

### C2 — ADJACENT / COMPONENT RISK

A component or similar mark exists in another category, or a weak technology use exists.

Investigate:

- strength;
- market reach;
- similarity;
- goods/services;
- search dominance;
- phonetic proximity.

Do NOT automatically reject.

### C3 — MATERIAL CATEGORY CONFLICT

Active company/product/software identity is substantially similar and operates in:

- AI;
- agent infrastructure;
- developer infrastructure;
- cloud;
- runtime;
- SDK/API;
- enterprise software;
- closely related technology services.

Normally reject unless evidence clearly shows the whole mark remains commercially distinct and professionally reviewable.

### C4 — DOMINANT / CONFUSING IDENTITY

The existing identity is so strong, similar, or category-adjacent that the proposed mark would plausibly be confused with it.

Reject.

### C5 — PROFESSIONAL / LEGAL BLOCK

Authoritative or professional clearance identifies a material legal obstacle.

Reject.

**Important:** C1 and C2 are investigation states, not automatic rejection states.

---

## 4. COMPONENT-COLLISION RULE — REVISED

Earlier cycles treated component collisions too aggressively.

The revised rule:

### Multi-word names

Test each component independently, but do not reject merely because a component is occupied.

Ask:

- Is the component famous?
- Is it dominant in AI/software?
- Does it create a strong semantic association?
- Does it materially weaken the whole-mark identity?
- Does it create likely confusion?
- Is it a common word with many unrelated uses?
- Can the whole combination create a clearly distinct identity?

### One-word names

Test:

- obvious morphemes;
- meaningful roots;
- phonetic neighbors;
- likely spelling variants.

Do not reject solely because a substring appears elsewhere.

A substring is not automatically a trademark conflict.

---

## 5. BRANDABLE > PERFECTLY STERILE

The naming target should prioritize:

- premium;
- elegant;
- simple;
- modern;
- memorable;
- pronounceable;
- globally workable;
- Indonesian-friendly;
- English-friendly;
- visually strong;
- easy to type;
- infrastructure-grade;
- extensible;
- durable beyond today's AI terminology.

The name may be:

- abstract;
- invented;
- adapted from real lexical material;
- a natural compound;
- a distinctive whole mark.

It does NOT need to have an empty Google result.

It does NOT need every component to be unused.

It does NOT need an exact .com.

It does NOT need zero social mentions.

It DOES need a reasonable path to ownership and commercial differentiation.

---

## 6. NAMING TERRITORY

Future cycles should deliberately explore names in the territory of examples such as:

- Kaervax;
- Kaivost;
- Calven-style forms;

without copying them.

The desired construction characteristics are:

- compact;
- confident;
- premium;
- neutral;
- abstract enough for infrastructure;
- not obviously AI;
- not obviously technical;
- not fantasy-coded;
- not pharma-coded;
- not surname-coded;
- not geographically locked;
- stable when spoken;
- credible as a company;
- credible as a platform.

Do not turn this into another forced pseudo-word generator.

The name should feel **intentional**, not algorithmically random.

---

## 7. STRATEGIC BRAND LOCK IS NOT LEGAL CLEARANCE

The program has three separate states:

### INTERNAL STRATEGIC SURVIVOR

The research indicates that the whole mark is commercially plausible and no C3/C4/C5 blocker is currently identified.

### PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE REQUIRED

The candidate is strong enough to justify trademark/domain/company review.

### FINAL BRAND LOCK

Only after the required professional/legal checks are completed to the project's chosen launch scope.

Never say:

- legally safe;
- trademark safe;
- globally clear;
- completely unused.

Use:

**STRATEGIC SURVIVOR — PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE REQUIRED**

when professional clearance remains.

---

## 8. PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE GATE

Professional review should focus on the actual intended commercial scope:

- software;
- SaaS;
- AI-agent infrastructure;
- developer platform;
- APIs/SDKs;
- cloud/runtime services;
- related technical services.

Prioritize:

- Indonesia;
- United States;
- European Union;

and additional markets when strategically relevant.

Use:

- DJKI/PDKI;
- USPTO;
- EUIPO;
- WIPO;
- UK IPO;
- relevant national sources.

The purpose is not to demand global certainty.

The purpose is to determine whether there is a material barrier to using and protecting the mark in the intended markets.

---

## 9. DOMAIN AND SOCIAL STANDARD

Domain and social checks are practical ownership checks, not identity purity tests.

An unavailable exact .com does not automatically kill a candidate.

Assess:

- active owner;
- commercial strength;
- asking price;
- brand conflict;
- viable alternative domain;
- Indonesian domain strategy;
- social handle practicality.

Likewise, an occupied social handle is not automatically a brand conflict.

Distinguish:

**IDENTITY CONFLICT** from **HANDLE AVAILABILITY**.

---

## 10. PHONETIC STANDARD

The name should be reasonably stable when spoken in Indonesian and English.

Prefer:

- one obvious primary reading;
- manageable syllable count;
- spelling that can be recovered after hearing;
- no embarrassing local meaning;
- no strong occupied homophone in technology.

Do not reject minor accent variation.

Do reject severe ambiguity or a homophone that materially changes commercial identity.

---

## 12. ADVERSARIAL QUESTION

Do not ask:

> “Can I find any reason to reject this?”

Ask:

> **“Is there a material, evidence-based reason this whole mark should NOT be used for this intended category?”**

Then classify the evidence.

This prevents trivial collisions from becoming terminal.

---

## 13. CANDIDATE FUNNEL

A practical cycle should target:

- 100–250 raw candidates;
- 20–40 screened;
- 5–12 serious finalists;
- 1–3 strategic survivors.

The purpose of broad generation is to discover viable territory, not to maximize rejection counts.

A cycle may end with a strategic survivor even when professional clearance is pending.

Do not continue generating indefinitely once a credible survivor exists.

---

## 14. NO-FORCED-WINNER STILL APPLIES

This standard does NOT mean:

“always choose something.”

Reject when there is:

- C3 material category conflict that cannot be reasonably separated;
- C4 dominant/confusing identity;
- C5 professional/legal block;
- severe pronunciation failure;
- severe negative meaning;
- clear consumer/product dominance that would confuse the intended category;
- no credible commercial identity path.

The change is only this:

> **Do not convert every incidental or component-level collision into a terminal failure.**

---

## 15. CYCLE DECISION MODEL

### Zero credible survivors

**NO BRAND LOCK**

Change methodology and continue.

### One or more credible strategic survivors

**STRATEGIC SURVIVOR — PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE REQUIRED**

Stop broad generation unless additional evidence is needed.

### Professional clearance pending

**NO FINAL BRAND LOCK — PROFESSIONAL CLEARANCE PENDING**

Do not pretend this is legal clearance.

### Professional clearance supports one candidate

Proceed to final lock according to the project's legal/commercial process.

---

## 16. WHAT CHANGES FROM CYCLES 015–020

The following historical rules remain useful:

- evidence-first research;
- collision research;
- registry screening;
- linguistic screening;
- developer ecosystem screening;
- adversarial testing;
- no fake availability claims;
- no fake legal clearance;
- no cosmetic rescue of failed names;
- no random pseudo-word generation;
- no forced winner.

The following rule is REVOKED:

> “One terminal component collision automatically rejects the whole mark.”

Replace it with:

> **“A component collision becomes terminal only when it materially threatens the whole-mark identity or intended commercial use.”**

---

## 17. AUTONOMOUS EXECUTION

Every cycle must:

READ → GENERATE → SCREEN → CONTEXTUALIZE → DEEP-CHECK → CLASSIFY RISK → PRESERVE OR REJECT → CLEARANCE HANDOFF → WRITE → VERIFY

Do not ask the owner to choose candidates when the cycle has already defined the methodology.

Do not stop merely because some candidates fail.

Do not manufacture certainty.

---

## 18. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The strategic naming philosophy is:

> **Distinctive enough to own.  
> Clear enough to understand.  
> Strong enough to build.  
> Defensible enough to clear.  
> Then use it until the market makes it unmistakably yours.**

This is the active naming doctrine for the AI Agent Infrastructure project.
