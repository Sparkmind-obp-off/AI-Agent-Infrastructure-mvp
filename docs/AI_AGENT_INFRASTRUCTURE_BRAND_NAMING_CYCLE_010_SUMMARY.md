## 0. OBJECTIVE

After nine consecutive naming cycles ended without a brand lock, Cycle 010 was mandated to test whether the bottleneck is (a) creativity or (b) actual availability and clearance of naming assets. Two routes were run in parallel:

- **Track A — Acquisition / Existing-Asset Scout:** locate parked, dormant, or explicitly-for-sale naming assets already in the market instead of inventing another mark.
- **Track B — Clearance-Constrained New-Name Protocol:** generate a small, tightly-constrained set of new candidates using a materially different architecture from Cycles 001–009, with early collision destruction and no rescue after hard-gate failure.

The core question, per Section 24 of the execution prompt, was to be answered on evidence, not forced.

**Halront.**
- No corporate, software, pharmaceutical, or fictional hit observed in reachable public sources. These are not real collisions.
- **However, adversarial destruction triggers (Section 15):**
  - **"Hal-" opening carries strong fictional-AI association** — HAL 9000 (2001: A Space Odyssey) is the archetypal-but-cautionary fictional AI, extensively cited in AI-safety and AI-agent discourse. For an AI-agent-infrastructure master brand, opening with "Hal-" imports the exact wrong cultural anchor (an AI-that-turns-hostile) into every developer/enterprise conversation.
  - **Indonesian phonetic parse**: "hal" is a very common Indonesian word meaning "matter, case, thing, concerning" (e.g., "hal ini penting"). Indonesian speakers will parse "Halront" as compound "hal + ront", where "ront" is not a meaningful Indonesian morpheme — producing a linguistically incomplete parse and a memorable-but-slightly-wrong feel (Section 8 phonetic test).
  - Search-sparse ≠ available (Cycle 004 lesson).
- **Verdict**: while there is no observed corporate/pharma/surname collision, Halront fails the adversarial "Hal- → HAL 9000 → fictional evil AI" test decisively for an AI-infrastructure master brand, and additionally has a soft Indonesian parse penalty. **REJECT**.
